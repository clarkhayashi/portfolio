Oops All In combines party mini games with a shared betting system. But how does the pot actually work? Share the room code and everyone begins with exactly 100 pretend chips. Before the prompt is revealed, every player must commit 5 chips into the center. Then you see the game and the betting begins. You match, raise, or fold. Got a short stack? You can still play, but you only win the exact portion you matched. The stakes escalate fast. From round 3, eligible games unlock the option to go all-in. Once bets lock, you actually play the mini game. Answering, drawing, or drafting to claim that pot. Some cooperative or bluffing games, like Same Brain and Who's Faking, skip betting entirely for a fixed 5-chip entry. That massive central pot pays out to the mini-game winner. The remaining chips are tallied and the board clears for the next hand. And after you've played through your chosen number of rounds, the player holding the most pretend chips wins the entire session.
